**封面文字**

血糖仪 × Apple Watch × 飞书群

**正文**

很多人的健康管理，都是从三件很普通的东西开始：一只血糖仪、一块 Apple Watch，和一个有人互相提醒的飞书群。

问题是，数据越来越多，建议却常常越来越泛。更重要的是，群聊适合陪伴，却不适合承载每个人的隐私。你不该为了得到一句建议，就把自己的原始健康数据摊在群里。

所以我们做了 FitCrew V2.0。

FitCrew 是产品品牌；BodyOS 是那个陪你把“知道”变成“今天能做什么”的私人生活方式教练；Moticlaw 负责管理与通道。它可以在你选择授权时连接 Apple Health、Apple Watch 和 Yuwell CGM 这类数据路径，但不连接也完全可以使用。

这里最重要的不是“接了多少数据”，而是边界：群里只保留低敏、固定的行为提示；真正个性化的讨论留在私聊。原始健康数值不会被拿到群里展示，教练也不是用来做诊断、开药或替代医生的。

如果两个人一起用，设备和每个健康类别的授权也要各自独立：一次性配对、不可跨用户上传、换设备会轮换关联，授权可以撤回。这不是炫技，而是健康数据应有的基本尊重。

我们已经完成 Python / 策略、Swift Core 和 iOS Simulator 的绿色 CI 验证，生产 TLS 与健康检查端点也已验证。它还不是“已经上线 TestFlight、人人可装”的故事：外部 TestFlight 分发和薛程的真机验收仍在后续里。

我们更愿意把 BodyOS 看成一个安静的同伴：不制造焦虑，不夸大数据，只在合适的时候，把复杂的日常变成一个小而可持续的动作。

### 微信公众号

**标题**

**导语**

健康管理最难的部分，往往不是缺少设备或知识，而是如何在日复一日的生活里，得到一条恰好能做、又不牺牲隐私的提醒。FitCrew V2.0 从一个很现实的起点出发：把血糖仪、Apple Watch 和飞书群里已有的陪伴，整理成一个有边界的私人健康教练体验。

#### 1. 不是再建一个“打卡群”

群聊很擅长让人不那么孤单：有人提醒，有人一起开始。但当话题进入睡眠、活动或血糖，群并不适合承接每个人的具体细节。我们不想要求用户以公开数据交换陪伴感。

#### 2. 三个名字，各做一件事

FitCrew 是产品品牌；BodyOS 是面向用户的私人生活方式教练；Moticlaw 负责管理与通道编排。这样的分工让体验可以保持简单：用户面对的是一个能理解日常节奏、给出小行动的教练，而不是一套必须研究的系统。

#### 3. 数据可以连接，也可以不连接

Apple Health 和 Apple Watch 的授权是可选的。用户在授权后，可通过独立设备与当前同意状态接入相应健康类别；Yuwell CGM 也在同一类受控接入路径内。选择不授权，并不意味着无法参与 FitCrew。

更关键的是，原始健康数值不是群聊内容。系统以加密方式处理原始数值和日特征；教练在需要时使用的是去标识化日级汇总、质量状态与约束，而不是把一整天的原始轨迹交给对话。

#### 4. 群里只有陪伴，私聊才有个性化

在飞书群里，FitCrew 只允许固定、低敏的行为提示，不展示原始健康数据、私聊内容或跨群信息。需要讨论目标、授权或个人情境时，BodyOS 在私聊中服务。这个边界看起来克制，却让“在一起”不等于“被看见一切”。

#### 5. 两个人使用，也要像两套独立空间

V2.0 为双用户设计了一次性配对和独立绑定：设备、用户和每个健康类别的同意分别对应；设备不会被另一位用户接管，跨用户上传会被拒绝。换设备时会轮换关联，授权也可以撤回。隐私不是一段说明文字，而应当是系统默认做出的动作。

#### 6. 先证明边界能跑起来，再把话说大

当前，我们已验证 Python / 策略、Swift Core 和 iOS Simulator CI，生产 TLS 与部署健康检查端点也已验证。已部署端点报告的版本是 `v2.0.0-alpha.1`。这证明的是工程交付和保护边界正在工作，不是对健康结果的保证。

外部 TestFlight 分发尚未完成，薛程的真机验收也尚未完成；我们不会把准备中的步骤写成已发生的发布，更不会把一个未来可能执行的 16 天实验写成已经得到结论。

**Headline**

From a glucose meter, an Apple Watch, and a Feishu group to a private health coach

**Subhead**

FitCrew V2.0: Keep companionship in the group, and personalization and privacy in your hands

**Lead**

The hardest part of health management is often not a lack of devices or knowledge. It is receiving a prompt that is practical for today without giving up privacy in the process. FitCrew V2.0 starts from a very real place: it organizes the support already around a glucose meter, an Apple Watch, and a Feishu group into a private health-coach experience with clear boundaries.

#### 1. Not another check-in group

Groups are good at making people feel less alone: someone reminds you, and someone starts alongside you. But when the topic turns to sleep, activity, or glucose, a group is not the right container for personal detail. We do not want people to trade public data for a sense of support.

#### 2. Three names, three jobs

FitCrew is the product brand. BodyOS is the user-facing private lifestyle coach. Moticlaw manages administration and channel orchestration. That division keeps the experience simple: a person meets a coach that understands daily rhythms and suggests small actions, not a system they have to study.

#### 3. Data can connect—or stay disconnected

Apple Health and Apple Watch authorization are optional. When a person grants permission, the relevant health categories can be connected through a dedicated device and current consent; Yuwell CGM follows the same kind of controlled path. Choosing not to authorize does not exclude someone from FitCrew.

More importantly, raw health values are not group content. The service handles raw values and daily features in encrypted form. When coaching needs data, it uses de-identified daily aggregates, quality state, and constraints—not a day's raw trace handed to a conversation.

#### 4. Groups are for companionship; DMs are for personalization

In Feishu groups, FitCrew permits only fixed, low-sensitivity behavior prompts. It does not expose raw health data, DM content, or cross-group information. Goals, authorization, and personal context belong in a DM with BodyOS. The boundary is deliberately restrained: being together should not mean being visible in every detail.

#### 5. Two people should still have two independent spaces

V2.0 uses one-time pairing and independent bindings for two people: the person, device, and each health-category consent map separately. A device cannot be taken over by another person, and cross-user uploads are rejected. Replacing a device rotates its association, and authorization can be withdrawn. Privacy should be a system default, not just a sentence in a policy.

#### 6. Prove the boundary works before making bigger claims

Python / policy, Swift Core, and iOS Simulator CI are currently verified, along with production TLS and the deployed health endpoint. The deployed endpoint reports `v2.0.0-alpha.1`. That demonstrates engineering delivery and protective boundaries—not a guaranteed health outcome.

External TestFlight distribution is not complete, and Xue Cheng's physical-device acceptance is not complete. We will not present preparation as a completed release, or a possible future 16-day experiment as if it had already produced a conclusion.

**Closing CTA**

If you believe health management should involve less public performance and more respectful companionship, follow FitCrew for what comes next. We will keep bringing verifiable engineering progress, explicit privacy boundaries, and genuinely doable small actions into the product. BodyOS is a lifestyle coach, not medical diagnosis, treatment, or medication advice; for symptoms or medical concerns, consult a qualified clinician.
